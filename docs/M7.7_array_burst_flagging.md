# M7.7 — Array-common burst flagging (core / arm autocorrelation sums)

Status: **implemented and tested on n03** (2026-09-09, observing
stopped). Measured results are in "What the node actually said" below;
they moved two defaults and refuted two of my own estimates.

## The problem

Every detector in the deployed flagger runs *inside a single antenna*,
and each needs a reference that the signal does not also move:

| detector | its discriminant | during bursts | when quiet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bandpass outlier | largest channel outlier within one antenna | 3.05 | 3.09 |
| group outlier | largest antenna outlier across the array | 38.1 | 38.9 |
| spectral kurtosis | fraction of cells fired | 2.98% | 2.92% |

None of them moves — by construction. A broadband burst lifts every
channel at once, so the bandpass median rises with it. It lifts every
antenna at once, so the group median rises with it. And it fills whole
2.097 ms accumulations, so the voltages stay Gaussian *within* an
accumulation, which is the only thing SK asks about. (Group-outlier's
38 in both columns is the permanently dead antennas, which is what it
is really there for.)

Measured on `260812imek sb02`, whose bursts reach 1.4% of the band
power and occupy 5.5% of the samples:

| how the data are averaged | burst significance |
| --- | --- |
| 1 antenna, 1 channel, 2.097 ms | 0.11 |
| **1 antenna, 1 channel, 134 ms cube (what the flagger tests)** | **0.05** |
| 1 antenna, all 384 channels, 2.097 ms | 2.24 |
| all 96 antennas, 1 channel, 2.097 ms | 1.04 |
| **all 96 antennas, all 384 channels, 2.097 ms** | **13.3** |

A factor of 270. This is not a threshold that needs lowering — the
signal is simply not present at a detectable level in the cell being
tested.

Full write-up and plots: `dsa_rfi_offline/out/DSA110_RFI_excision_report.pdf`
(pages 8–11).

## What was added

`dsart.rfi.array_burst` forms the one statistic the existing chain does
not: **a short-lived rise in total power relative to the array's own
recent history.**

### Scales

**Time: 2.097 ms (M = 64), 64 decisions per cube.** `compute_autos`
already builds `s1[64]` (`[64, 96, 384, 2]`, 18.9 MB) on the GPU to
feed SK, so the detector is a reduction over a resident tensor — no
new moments, no second pass over the voltages. Going finer would need
a new accumulation; going coarser is what the present chain does, and
is what dilutes the signal ~20-fold.

**Frequency: two scales.** Detection on the full 11.72 MHz sub-band
sum, which is where the SNR is. Decision on 24 coarse bins of 16
channels (0.488 MHz): one (82-antenna core, 16-channel, 2.097 ms) cell
has ~0.46% fractional noise against a ~1.45% burst, so each bin is
~3 σ — about the finest binning at which this burst class is still
individually visible.

Each corr node sees only its own chgroup, so "broadband" means
broadband *within one sub-band*. Cross-node coordination would need
the transport fabric and cannot fit the latency budget.

### Groups

Split from the (E, N) positions the cal blob already carries. The core
is an offset T: **47 antennas** on an E-W line at constant N (zero
spread) and **35** on an N-S line at constant E (zero spread), plus 14
outriggers at station > 102. Stable for any `arm_tol_m` from 5 to 50 m.

| group | ants | median σ | peak σ | burst % of own power |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| all | 96 | 14.5 | 39.5 | 1.42 |
| **core** (default) | 82 | **15.4** | 42.8 | 1.45 |
| EW arm | 47 | 14.6 | 57.8 | **1.86** |
| NS arm | 35 | 6.0 | 31.3 | **0.81** |
| outriggers | 14 | 2.0 | 13.8 | — |

The E-W / N-S asymmetry is a **local vs far-field discriminant**: a
far-field source lights both arms in proportion to collecting area, so
2.3× says near-field, and roughly where. It is published as a
diagnostic, **not** used in the firing decision.

### The gate

A sample fires when the flag group's band-summed power exceeds
`detect_k` σ (default 6) **and** the excess is present in at least
`occupancy_min` (default 0.75) of the coarse bins.

The occupancy term is what protects FRBs. Across one 11.72 MHz
sub-band at 1.4 GHz the dispersion sweep is `0.035 · DM` ms, so in a
single 2.097 ms sample an FRB lights ~60% of the band at DM 100, ~20%
at DM 300, ~6% at DM 1000.

**That analytic picture is not what the array does, and the difference
set the default.** Injecting dispersed pulses into a real dump on n03,
the occupancy a sweep actually reaches is higher than the fraction of
band it lights — 0.75 at DM 100, 0.46 at DM 300, 0.29 at DM 1000 —
because the wings of a bright sweep still clear the per-bin threshold.
Worse, it **rises with brightness**, so the margin is not
amplitude-independent. At the originally-chosen `occupancy_min = 0.75`
a DM-100 pulse at +300% band power reached exactly 0.75 and **was
flagged**. The default is now **0.90**, which clears every dispersed
case measured while a dispersion-free burst reaches 1.00 even at +5%.

**A genuinely zero-DM broadband event would fire.** Those currently
rail at the `dm_min = 100` search floor and are classified
extragalactic by construction, so removing them is arguably a second
win — but it is a real consequence and worth stating.

### Gain normalisation and dead antennas

Each antenna is divided by its own running per-(channel, pol) mean
before summing. Dead antennas must additionally be **excluded**, not
down-weighted: in the offline study, leaving them in put the all-96
null at 0.978 ± 0.026 instead of 1.000 ± 0.0013 — a 20 σ error in the
very quantity the threshold is set against. The 0/0 this creates for a
dead antenna is also the bug the first draft shipped with; it now has
a strictly-positive relative floor and a test that fails on NaN.

## How it is deployed

### Fast path — MONITOR ONLY

`--rfi-array-burst-mode monitor` (the shipped default). The detector
runs, the results reach the RFI monitor page, and **nothing is
excised.**

This is not timidity. The measured real-time margin is much thinner
than the code comments claim. Sampling `/mon/corr_rt/<cn>/corr_fast`
across the fleet on 2026-09-08, 88 samples over 11 nodes:

```
p50 = 126.9 ms   p90 = 138.6 ms   max = 144.2 ms
block period = 134.218 ms  ->  7.3 ms of headroom at p50, negative at p90
```

`n_drop` was 0 and every `fada` ring was empty (70/70 free), so the
pipeline is keeping up — the overruns are transients absorbed by the
9.4 s ring — but the code's "~110 ms p50" comment is stale by ~17 ms.

The detector is designed to that budget:

* it reduces `s1[64]`, already resident, into `[64, 5, 24, 2]`;
* it adds **no host synchronisation** — no `.item()`, `.any()` or
  `bool()` on a device tensor anywhere in `detect()`. Confirmed on the
  node: with the GPU deliberately backlogged, `detect()` returns in
  1.8 ms while the queue takes 584 ms to drain;
* the time-resolved excision reuses the **existing** `mul_` over the
  voltage cube rather than adding a second pass, by growing the
  broadcast `keep` tensor from `(384, 1, 2, 1, 1, 96)` to
  `(384, 1, 2, 64, 1, 96)` — 0.15 MB to 9.4 MB.

**I estimated ~0.2–0.4 ms. The measurement says otherwise** (2080 Ti,
production shapes):

| | p50 |
| --- | --- |
| `ArrayBurstDetector.detect()` | **1.6 ms** |
| zero-fill, cube mask only (pre-existing) | 10.2 ms |
| zero-fill, + time-resolved mask | 13.3 ms |
| **added by monitor mode** | **+1.6 ms — 22% of the 7.3 ms headroom** |
| **added by flag mode** | **+4.7 ms — 64% of the headroom** |

So the "negligible, it folds into the existing pass" claim was wrong:
the richer broadcast costs **+3.1 ms**, a 30% increase on a zero-fill
that itself costs 10.2 ms (not the "~150 µs" its own comment claims —
that comment is as stale as the block-time one).

Optimisation was attempted and did not help. `torch.compile` is
**0.87×**, i.e. slower, because dynamo re-guards every cube on the
rebound `self._gain`. Reformulating the antenna+channel contraction as
a single fused einsum or bmm is **2× slower** than the present
multiply-bin-einsum. Profiling shows the named ops total only ~0.5 ms;
the rest is launch overhead across ~35 small kernels, which nothing
short of a custom kernel or CUDA graphs will remove.

**Conclusion: monitor mode fits and ships. Flag mode does not fit the
fast path** at 64% of headroom, which independently confirms the
decision to keep it monitor-only there. The slow path is a different
budget and is where flag mode actually runs.

To arm it later: `--rfi-array-burst-mode flag`, or edit
`configs/dsart_pipeline_rt.yaml` and re-push.

### Slow path — ARMED

The slow correlator gains optional flagging: the full per-antenna
chain (SK, bandpass-outlier, group-outlier, SumThreshold,
`flagants.dat`) **plus** the array-burst detector in `flag` mode. Slow
visibilities are not searched for FRBs, so the risk that keeps the
fast path in monitor mode does not apply.

It is **off by default** — slow visibilities have always been emitted
raw so cal solutions can be derived downstream — and is toggled
fleet-wide from the dashboard Control tab, which writes
`/cnf/corr_slow_rfi`. Each `corr_slow` process polls that key every
5 s in a daemon thread, so the toggle takes effect without a restart
and a node that restarts picks up the current state.

**Turning it on changes what every downstream calibration sees.**

#### A landmine worth knowing about

`dsart.rfi.sk._mc_sk_thresholds` draws a `(1e6, M)` float64 array —
**32.8 GB at M = 4096** — so the first block that flags is a
multi-minute, multi-gigabyte event, not the "< 100 ms per M" its
docstring claims. `corr_fast` absorbs this in its pre-sentinel
dummy-block warmup (Phase 8b), which is why nobody notices.

`corr_slow` cannot do the same, because flagging here is normally off
at startup and may be switched on hours later. Paying it inline would
stall the block loop long enough to fill `fada` (70 blocks = 9.4 s),
and fada back-pressure reaches the SNAPs. So `SkWarmup` runs it in a
daemon thread and flagging is held off until it completes — a few
unflagged blocks at the moment of the toggle is a far better failure
mode than dropped UDP packets. `n_blocks_flag_deferred` in the run
summary counts them.

Memory matters as much as time here: `corr_slow` and `corr_fast` share
a node, so an unconditional warm at startup would have both processes
reaching for 33 GB at once. Nothing is allocated unless flagging is
actually enabled.

(The 33 GB allocation itself is pre-existing and out of scope here.
The offline twin at `dsa_rfi_offline` solves it with chunked row-wise
generation — bit-identical, max|diff| = 0 — plus a disk cache, taking
the per-block cost from 660 s to 6 s. Worth porting into
`dsart.rfi.sk` separately.)

### The Galactic HI guard

The offline study found the online flagger **excising Galactic HI at
up to 99.5% occupancy.** That is correct on the fast path (HI is fully
resolved out — 28% of its channel's power but `|V| = 0.003`, exactly
the clean-channel floor) and wrong on the slow path, where the
visibilities go to UVH5 for imaging and calibration.

So when slow-path flagging is on, a protected band is carved out that
no detector may flag: **−350 to +200 km/s radio velocity =
1419.458–1422.064 MHz.** At the DSA-110 channel plan that is **86
channels, all in chgroup 6** (local channels 209–294) — a no-op on the
other fifteen nodes, which is worth knowing before wondering why the
guard "does nothing" on the node you are testing on.

## What the node actually said

Run on **n03** on 2026-09-09 with observing stopped, from a clean
clone of the branch, using the production cal blob
(`dsa110-xengine/utils/antennas.out`) and a real 4.53 GB voltage dump
(`250924mptq_sb00`, 15 blocks, chgroup 0, 1487–1499 MHz).

**Grouping against the real array.** The split reproduces the offline
geometry exactly from the production cal blob: **47 / 35 / 82 / 14**,
arms partitioning the core with no overlap and no remainder, and
identical for every `arm_tol_m` from 5 to 50 m. The E-W arm spans
396.9 m of East with 0.3 m of North spread; the N-S arm spans 293.9 m
of North with 0.2 m of East spread. This is the check the synthetic
unit tests could not make.

**Null on real sky data.** Over all 15 blocks, **0 of 832** samples
fired. Core significance ran at a median of 0.40–0.56 with a maximum
of ~4.0 — comfortably inside the 6 σ threshold, on data that also
contains real RFI (the per-antenna chain flagged 8.5% of cells over
the same blocks, consistent with the 3.9–10.6% the offline study
measured across five events).

**FRB safety** — pulses injected along a real dispersion track, 1 ms
wide, as a fractional power boost applied to every antenna:

| DM | occupancy reached | +30% | +100% | +300% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 | 0.75 | survives | survives | survives |
| 300 | 0.46 | survives | survives | survives |
| 1000 | 0.25 | survives | survives | survives |

and the control, a dispersion-free burst of the same construction:

| DM | occupancy | +5% | +15% | +30% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1.00 | **flagged** | **flagged** | **flagged** |

For scale, +300% means the array's total band power quadrupling —
against a ~9000 Jy SEFD that is a ~27 kJy source, some three orders of
magnitude brighter than the brightest FRB on record. The gate holds
well past anything physical.

**The first version of this test was wrong, and the control caught
it.** The initial injection boosted one native sample (32.768 µs) per
channel — 1/64th of an accumulation — so everything "survived"
trivially. It was the DM = 0 control *failing to fire* that exposed
it; without that control the run would have been read as a pass.
Widening the pulse to a realistic 1 ms is what produced the numbers
above, and what revealed the amplitude-dependent occupancy that moved
the default from 0.75 to 0.90.

**Memory.** n03 carries a known failing DIMM (uncorrectable ECC on
P2-DIMMD1, escalating to CPU IERR since 2026-08-20) and the SK
threshold Monte Carlo transiently allocates ~33 GB, so the SEL was
checked before and after. Baseline: last memory event 2026-08-20, 19
days of clean uptime, all EDAC counters zero. No new entries appeared
during testing. The node has 180 GB, so the allocation was
comfortable — this would not have been true on h23.

## The monitor

* **shm ABI v2.** The array-burst section is appended after the
  existing body, and its metadata rides in six of the header's twenty
  reserved uint64 words, so **every pre-existing field keeps its
  offset**. The reader accepts v1 and v2, treating v1 as "no group
  data", so a rolling deploy degrades instead of throwing. 96,040 B
  per record at the production op-point (184 kB → 280 kB).
* **New dashboard tab: Array sums** (`/arraysum`). The core and each
  arm presented as extra antennas: band-summed significance at
  2.097 ms with the fired samples marked, the E-W vs N-S scatter
  against the 1:1 far-field line, gain-normalised group spectra over
  the full band, and a per-group table.
* **etcd**: `/mon/corr_rt/<cn>/rfi` gains an `array_burst` block with
  mode, per-group fired fraction, and the arm ratio. The arm ratio is
  computed **over the fired samples only** — a peak over a whole
  window measures the largest noise excursion in ~1000 samples, which
  is both larger than the real asymmetry and insensitive to it.

Bit 5 does **not** mean the same thing as bits 0–4. The array-burst
verdict is time-resolved, so it cannot be folded into the cube-wide
`[NANTS, NCHAN, NPOL]` mask without discarding the time resolution
that is the whole point. Bit 5 marks cells in which *at least one*
2.097 ms sample was excised; the mask itself travels separately in
`FlagBlockResult.array_burst` and the caller applies it. It is set
only in `flag` mode.

## On-node test plan

Steps 0-6 were run on n03 on 2026-09-09; results above. Steps 7-8
(slow-path enable on a live fleet, and the rolling-deploy check)
remain, and want the fleet actually running.

0. **A warning about running the wider suite on h23.** Do not. Any
   test that flags a real block pulls in the 32.8 GB SK Monte Carlo
   described above; a run of `tests/test_corr_fast_integration.py`
   reached 31.3 GB RSS in uninterruptible sleep with 4 GB of swap in
   use, on the box that also runs `dsa_monitor`, `dsart_c2` and
   `dsart_c3`. It had to be killed. On the corr node the memory is
   there and this is a non-issue; on h23 either stay with the three
   files below (which never reach M = 4096 with the real MC) or stub
   `dsart.rfi.sk._mc_sk_thresholds` with `gaussian_sk_thresholds`,
   which is enough to exercise the wiring but NOT the thresholds.

1. **Unit tests on the node** (they are CPU-only but the env there is
   the real one):
   `pytest tests/test_array_burst.py tests/test_array_burst_integration.py tests/test_hi_guard.py`

2. **Cost, on the GPU.** The number that decides whether the fast path
   can ever be armed. Replay a voltage dump through
   `bench/_corr_fast_replay.py` with `--rfi-array-burst-mode off` and
   then `monitor`, and compare `ms_per_block_p50/p99`. Budget: the
   delta must be well under 1 ms, against 7.3 ms of headroom.

3. **Cost of the armed path.** Same, with `flag`. This exercises the
   larger `keep` tensor. Expect ≈ the `monitor` cost plus tens of µs;
   if it is milliseconds, the `mul_` is being done twice or the view
   is copying.

4. **Detector behaviour on real data.** Run `monitor` on a node for a
   few minutes and read `/arraysum`. Expect: quiet data fires at
   ≈ 0 (the null test says < 1e-3), group spectra sitting at 1.0,
   `n_live` matching the known dead-antenna count.

5. **Response to a known event.** Replay `260812imek` (sb02 has the
   bursts) and confirm the core fires at a median ~15 σ with an
   E-W/N-S ratio near 2.3. This is the end-to-end check that the
   production path reproduces the offline measurement.

6. **FRB safety.** Inject a dispersed pulse with the existing
   injection machinery at DM 300 and 1000 and confirm `fired` stays 0
   while the injection is clearly present in the search output.
   **Do not arm the fast path until this passes.**

7. **Slow path.** Enable via the Control tab. Watch first for
   `slow-RFI: warming the SK threshold cache in the background` and
   then `SK thresholds ready for M=[...] in N s` — time it and check
   the node's RSS while it runs, since that is the 33 GB draw. Confirm
   `n_blocks_flag_deferred` stops advancing once it completes, and
   that `fada` never backs up during the warm. Then confirm the log
   line
   `slow-RFI: ... 86 channels 209-294 of chgroup 6 are PROTECTED` on
   the chgroup-6 node and `inactive on this node` elsewhere, and check
   that `n_blocks_flagged` advances. Verify a UVH5 product still has
   HI intact.

8. **Rolling-deploy check.** With one node updated and the rest on v1,
   confirm `/arraysum` renders and the Antennas/RFI tab is unaffected.
